# arXiv 2204.03826

Title: Matrix multiplication via matrix groups
Year: 2022

### BCGPU Theorem 3.2 (Representation-theoretic barrier via n(G))

If S, T, U satisfy TPP in a finite nonabelian group G, then |S||T||U| <= |G|^{3/2}/n(G)^{1/2} + |G|, where n(G) is the second-smallest irreducible representation dimension. This is a fundamental barrier: groups where n(G) grows as a power of |G| cannot meet the packing bound. The sieve computes n(G) as a key invariant.

### BCGPU Corollary 3.4 (Lie type groups cannot prove omega = 2)

No group of Lie type can prove any bound on omega better than 2 + epsilon for an absolute constant epsilon > 0. This resolves a question from CU03 and rules out SL(n,q), Sp(2n,q), etc. as standalone hosts.

### BCGPU Theorem 3.6 (Normalizer barrier for subgroups)

If subgroups H_1, H_2, H_3 satisfy TPP in G with normalizer indices s_i = |N(H_i)|/|H_i|, then |H_1||H_2||H_3| <= |G|^{3/2}/(s_1 s_2 s_3)^{1/4}. Self-normalizing subgroups are therefore preferred, a structural criterion the sieve should track.

### BCGPU Corollary 3.8 (Center barrier)

|H_1||H_2||H_3| <= |G|^{3/2}/|Z(G)|^{1/2}. Groups with large center cannot yield good bounds. The sieve computes |Z(G)| as a primary invariant.

### BCGPU Proposition 4.3 (Lie exponent > 2 for all Lie groups)

Every Lie group G has omega(G) > 2, where omega(G) is the Lie exponent. This is the continuous analog of the packing bound barrier.

### BCGPU Theorem 4.9 (SL(n,R)^m Lie exponent)

The Lie exponent of SL(n,R)^m is at most 3m(n-1)/(m(n-1)-n), approaching 3 as m -> infinity. This is one of the best known constructions in the continuous setting.

### BCGPU Theorem 4.10 (Three conjugates of O(n,R) meet packing bound)

Three conjugates of O(n,R) in GL(n,R) satisfy the K-TPP and meet the packing bound. This construction evades the normalizer barrier via self-normalizing subgroups.

### BCGPU Question 4.2 (Is omega(G) <= omega for all Lie groups G?)

Open question: does the Lie exponent always bound the MM exponent? A positive answer would establish a continuous-to-discrete transfer principle.
